About the Author

Vahid Moslemi is a senior software engineer and software architect with more than ten years of experience designing and shipping complex, reliable cross-platform software in modern C++, Qt, and QML — across desktop (Windows and Linux), Android, and embedded Linux.

His work centers on reusable software platforms, SDKs, and HMI applications: a single, well-architected codebase engineered to behave identically on very different hardware, from a small embedded device to a desktop workstation. It is the kind of engineering where architecture actually bites — clean API and ABI boundaries, portability by design, and automated test coverage treated as a requirement rather than a nicety.

Areas of focus

  • Software architecture & design — design patterns, UML, and API/ABI design for libraries and SDKs other teams build on.
  • Modern C++ (C++17/20/23), Qt, and QML — safe, expressive, performance-minded code.
  • Cross-platform delivery — one codebase across Windows, Linux, Android, and embedded Linux.
  • Reusable platforms & HMI — reliable software for devices people depend on.
  • Safety & test automation — testability by design, with warnings-as-errors and coverage built in.

That background is why this book emphasizes what it does. When one codebase must run on a small embedded device and a desktop workstation, portability stops being a nice-to-have. When software ships inside devices that people rely on, safety and automated tests stop being optional. And when a platform is meant to be reused by many applications, API and SDK design becomes the difference between a library people build on and one they work around. Those are the priorities of the twenty-one days ahead.

He holds a Master's degree in Computer Engineering and a Bachelor's in Computer Hardware Engineering. He is an R&D-driven, self-directed learner and a collaborative team member — open to new challenges and always learning new skills. That last habit is why this book is shaped the way it is: everything is built up from the beginning, every concept arrives with something you can compile and run, and no step assumes knowledge that an earlier day didn't teach.

How This Book Is Written

This book is drafted with agentic AI and edited by a human. I guide the AI throughout the entire process, using my experience of more than ten years to review every piece of code, refine the text and examples, and verify all references. Every code listing is compiled and run before publication — with warnings enabled and treated as errors — and every printed output in these pages is real program output, not something typed by hand.

My goal is a well-structured, clear, and reliable open-source book that helps others learn modern C++ with confidence. But like every open-source project, it is the contribution of others that makes it a success. Automation plus a single reviewer is not the same as many eyes: please check everything and share your fixes or notes.

Corrections, portability reports ("this fails on my compiler"), and "I got lost in this paragraph" feedback are all equally welcome, at github.com/vahidmoslemi/modern-cpp-in-21-days/issues. New chapters are announced on LinkedIn.
